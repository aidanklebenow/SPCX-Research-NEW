## Thesis and business

Visa operates a global payments network connecting consumers, merchants and financial institutions. Its business depends on payment activity and associated services. [Company description](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/Visa-Fiscal-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)

**Judgment:** Visa is easier to value than the earlier SpaceX case because an established operating history permits a cash-generating starting point. Nevertheless, a strong business can offer an unattractive prospective return at a demanding purchase price.

## Management discussion: what is good

Fiscal Q3 2026 revenue rose **14% to $11.633B**; payment volume and processed transactions each grew **10%**, while cross-border volume excluding intra-Europe grew **12%**. GAAP EPS increased **10% to $2.97**. These operating trends support continued growth, although they do not establish future FCFF growth. [Q3 earnings release, operating highlights](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000103/q32026earningsrelease.htm)

## What is bad

Q3 client incentives increased **18%**, faster than net revenue, and GAAP operating expenses increased **19%**. These are reasons to question whether volume gains will translate proportionately into shareholder cash generation. [Q3 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000103/q32026earningsrelease.htm)

Nine-month operating cash flow fell from **$16.821B to $16.342B**. Management attributes the decline primarily to litigation payments, incentive payments and tax timing, partly offset by underlying growth. [June 10-Q, MD&A cash flow discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000104/v-20260630.htm)

## Advantages and challenges

**Interpretation:** A large established network can benefit from broad acceptance and repeated usage. Maintaining its economics requires continued investment, competitive services and dependable processing. Principal research risks are payment competition, incentive pressure, litigation, regulation, cybersecurity and weaker consumer or cross-border spending. Visa discusses these exposures in its [2025 10-K, Business and Risk Factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000089/v-20250930.htm). No legal outcome is assumed here.

## Recent performance against history

Fiscal-year revenue increased from **$32.653B in 2023** to **$35.926B in 2024** and **$40.000B in 2025**. That progression supports an established growth thesis. [2025 10-K, revenue history](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000089/v-20250930.htm)

**Judgment:** Recent operating expansion is favorable, but the divergence between earnings growth and cash receipts merits attention. Extrapolating EPS growth without examining incentives, taxes and working capital would weaken the valuation.

## Outlook and valuation

My forecast assumes five-year FCFF growth of **12%, 11%, 10%, 9%, and 8%**, a **9% WACC**, and **3% terminal growth**. These are analyst assumptions, not a quoted management outlook. Starting normalized FCFF is **$20.927B**, derived from TTM operating results with assumed tax and working-capital investment.

The reverse DCF requires approximately **19.37% annual FCFF growth for five years** to explain the reference price with the base discount and terminal assumptions. That hurdle is substantially above the modeled base cash-growth path. It is not a direct forecast of revenue growth.

## Consequential assumptions and unknowns

- The normalized starting cash flow must represent durable earning power; litigation and working-capital timing could make it too high or low.
- The 9% WACC is a scenario assumption that needs independent estimation.
- Terminal cash generation assumes reinvestment can sustain 3% perpetual growth. Most enterprise value comes from the terminal calculation.
- June cash, debt and as-converted shares are used as proxies at the September valuation date. Conversion mechanics, buybacks and employee dilution need a dated reconciliation.
- New acquisitions and individual future litigation settlements are not separately modeled.

## Research plan and decision triggers

1. Review the upcoming annual filing against the starting FCFF estimate, especially incentives and cash conversion.
2. Estimate WACC from dated market inputs and replace the scenario assumption.
3. Build revenue, operating margin and reinvestment forecasts that independently support the FCFF growth path.
4. Reconcile all participating shares, cash restrictions and material claims at the valuation date.
5. Compare the revised valuation with the share price and required margin of safety.

**Consider BUY** if a defensible updated DCF exceeds price by a sufficient margin, or the price falls materially while business fundamentals remain sound. A 20% cushion below the current base estimate is approximately **$201/share**, a review reference rather than an automatic buy rule.

**Remain WAIT** while the price requires growth or capital-cost assumptions that the evidence does not adequately support. Weaker cash conversion or impaired competitive economics would call for a lower valuation, not merely waiting for a lower price.
